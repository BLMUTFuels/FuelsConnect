BLM Utah Fire & Aviation worked closely with the Castle Valley community to reduce wildfire risk in an area naturally prone to fire due to frequent lightning and dense vegetation.

 

BLM employed a burn probability model to help guide the Castle Valley Wildfire Mitigation Project and pinpoint the areas most in need of treatment. Over 10,000 simulation fires were randomly ignited and were allowed to “burn” for 6 hours. This model was set to represent weather and vegetation conditions during an extreme fire season with a 20-mph wind from the southeast.  

 

With input from residents and technical experts, more than 2,300 acres have been treated to reduce fire intensity, improve ecological resilience, and enhance firefighter access and safety.  

 

Over the past several decades, juniper expansion has increased the risk of high-severity wildfires by displacing native vegetation and creating continuous fuel sources. Thinning young junipers and restoring mosaic vegetation patterns helps preserve native seedbeds, limit erosion, and support wildlife habitat. By partnering with local landowners, BLM Utah extended treatments beyond public land, building a landscape-level approach to wildfire mitigation.
