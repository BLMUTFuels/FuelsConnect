BLM continues work at the Pariette Wetlands Water Control Improvement Project, restoring habitat and reducing wildfire risk across 10,627 acres in the Pariette Wash Watershed, roughly 50 miles southwest of Vernal, Utah.

  • Fuels Reduction and Controlled Burns:
    1. Invasive vegetation—including dense stands of tamarisk and Russian olive—was removed to reduce hazardous fuels.
    2. Prescribed fire treatments are scheduled biannually, occurring during early and late winter windows to align with optimal burn conditions.
    3. Sediment was cleared from ditches and ponds to improve water flow and reduce dry, fire-prone buildup.
  • Wildfire Mitigation and Resilience:
    1. Treatments create natural fire breaks and reduce the potential for high-intensity wildfires in and around the wetland complex.
    2. Restored wet meadows and reestablished native vegetation help retain soil moisture and slow fire spread in dry conditions.
    3. Infrastructure upgrades—including dikes, culverts, and irrigation systems—support long-term water retention, helping buffer surrounding uplands during fire season.
  • Habitat Restoration:
    1. Enhanced wetland function benefits 22 ponds and 1,100 acres of native wet meadow.
    2. Supports a wide range of wildlife, including deer, elk, pronghorn, American white pelicans, sandhill cranes, monarch butterflies, bumble bees, and several bat species.
  • Collaborative Conservation:
    1. Builds on prior habitat efforts in the area (Projects 5287, 5703, and 6731).
    2. Future collaboration with UDWR and USFWS may include reintroducing native fish species once infrastructure is fully restored.

 

For more detailed information, refer to the Utah Watershed Restoration Initiative Report on the project.
